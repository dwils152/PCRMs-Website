PCRMs logo: transcription factors and DNA

Eight new directions built around what PCRMs is about: transcription factors binding DNA in clusters. Each mark is shown full-colour, on the teal tile, at 32 and 16px, and in the full logo. Refer to them by code, e.g. “T1”.

T1 Enhancer

Three different transcription factors bound side by side on DNA, bracketed as one module: the textbook picture of a CRM.

T2 Clamp

A transcription factor clamped around a double helix. The clamp also reads as the C of CRM.

T3 Leucine zipper

A TF dimer whose two arms grip the DNA, like a bZIP factor binding its site.

T4 Helix with sites

A double helix whose highlighted base pairs are the binding sites, bracketed as a module.

T5 Motif

A TF binding motif drawn as a sequence logo (A, C, G, T scaled by information) inside module brackets, sitting on DNA. The site already shows motif logos.

T6 TF tracks

Each track is a different TF’s ChIP-seq data; their binding sites line up in one column. That co-binding is the CRM.

T7 Zinc fingers

A zinc-finger TF resting on DNA: three fingers, each with its zinc ion, reading the sequence.

T8 P[CRM]s wordmark

The name itself carries the idea: brackets mark CRM as the module inside PCRMs, with a strand of DNA beneath it. The square mark is a bracketed P.
